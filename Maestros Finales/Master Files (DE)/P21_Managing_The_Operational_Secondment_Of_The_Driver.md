## Understanding the operational secondment of the driver

Before you define advanced rules, absences or Rostering calculations, you need to understand how **seconded** is left for each driver within the organization. In GoalBus, operational secondment is not based on a single field. It is built by combining three main coordinates:
1. **Deposit**
2. **Business unit**
3. **Working Group**

This combination defines where the person works, what division he belongs to and what type of tasks he can receive. It also conditions the visibility of the resource for planners and managers. fileciteturn39file3L1-L20

Use this quick start when you already have the driver template loaded and need to make sure that each person is located in the right operating context before moving on to rules and availability.

Before you start, make sure that:
1. You already loaded and checked the drivers at P20.
2. You know what deposits, units and groups use your operation.
3. You're clear which staff group will participate in the Rostering calculation.
4. You know that bad secondment can make a person ineligible even if it exists in the system.

For this quick start, use this reference case:

> **I will check that the drivers that will cover the L1 line are attached to the correct deposit, unit and task force before setting up rules and availability.**

To understand operational secondment:
1. It treats the **deposit** as the physical base location of the resource.
2. It treats **business unit** as the strategic or modal division to which the person belongs.
3. Treat the **working group** as the function that determines what type of tasks you can receive.
4. Use this reading rule:
   1. the deposit responds to **where he works**,
   2. the unit responds to **in which business or mode it operates**,
   3. the group responds to **what kind of work you can do**.
5. Do not mix these three concepts as if they were the same.

When you finish this section, you should be clear that operational secondment is a composite structure and not a single isolated attribute. fileciteturn39file1turn39file3

## Checking tank, unit and working group on driver profile

Once the logic is understood, you need to check how it is configured in the actual driver profile. These fields are part of the “structural DNA” of the employee and are the basis of their operating context. If they are poorly defined, the rear mapping is contaminated from the source. fileciteturn39file0turn39file2

Before starting this section, make sure that:
1. You already have drivers created in the template.
2. You know which driver or group you'll use as a sample.
3. You want to review the structural secondment, not yet a temporary assignment.

To review the secondment in the profile:
1. On the general list of drivers, open a person's profile.
2. Check the structural data sidebar.
3. Check at least:
   1. **Main deposit**
   2. **Business unit**
   3. **Working Group**
   4. **Area**, if your operation uses it
4. Confirms that those values coincide with the actual context where the person should work.
5. If a data is incorrect, update it in the profile.
6. Save the changes.
7. Repeat the review on multiple drivers to confirm that the template is consistent.

For the reference case, it finds that:
1. L1 drivers belong to the correct tank.
2. The business unit matches the expected mode or business.
3. The working group really corresponds to **Drivers** and not to another role.

When you finish this section, you should have revised the structural attachment of drivers who will participate in the calculation. fileciteturn39file1turn39file2

## Understanding the difference between main secondment, habilitation and assignment

Before you go on, you need to distinguish three concepts that are often confused:
1. **Principal secondment**
2. **Enabling**
3. **Transfer or temporary transfer**

The main adscription defines where the person belongs structurally. The habilitation responds to whether **may** work legally or technically in another context. The cession responds to where **He's really working.** for a temporary period. These three layers coexist, but do not mean the same. fileciteturn39file0turn39file4

Before starting this section, make sure that:
1. You've already checked the main secondment in the profile.
2. You know that some people can work outside of their main context.
3. You want to avoid misinterpretation between “belongs to”, “can work on” and “is working on”.

To correctly distinguish these concepts:
1. Use the **main secondment** to describe the basic structural context of the driver.
2. Use the **rating** to indicate that the driver can work in another tank, group or unit.
3. Use the **assignment** to indicate that the driver is temporarily moved to another context.
4. Do not use an assignment to correct a misdefined main secondment.
5. Don't use a rating like it's an active move.
6. Keep these questions as a guide:
   1. Where does this person belong? → main subscription
   2. Where could I work legally? → Enabling
   3. Where are you working right now? → cession

For the reference case, ask yourself:
1. The driver belongs to the North Depot?
2. Can you work in another warehouse if necessary?
3. Is it temporarily transferred to another base or is it still in its usual context?

When you finish this section, you should have a correct reading of the hierarchy between secondment, habilitation and assignment. fileciteturn39file0turn39file4

## Validating that the secondment allows to view and assign the driver correctly

Adscription not only serves to describe the driver’s profile. It also conditions how the system sees it and what tasks it can receive. An ill-adscribed person may be left out of the correct filter, appear in the wrong place or receive tasks that do not correspond to him. The opposite may also occur: that a valid person is hidden or ineligible by a poorly defined attachment. fileciteturn39file3L1-L20

Before continuing, make sure that:
1. You've already checked warehouse, unit and group in several profiles.
2. You understand the difference between secondment and assignment.
3. You're already clear which collective will participate in the next calculation.

To validate the operational impact of secondment:
1. Check which set of drivers should be visible for the context of your calculation.
2. Check that the right people appear under the right deposit, unit and group.
3. Check if there are drivers in the wrong group.
4. Check if there are drivers who should belong to the context and do not appear as such.
5. If you detect a secondment error, correct it before moving on to rules or availability.
6. Saves the final configuration of affected profiles.

For the reference case, make sure that:
1. Drivers who will cover L1 appear in the correct operating context.
2. They do not mix with groups that should not receive driving tasks.
3. The system could filter and assign only relevant personnel.

When you finish this section, you should have an operating secondment base that helps the system see and use the right people.

## Confirming that operational secondment is already ready for the next layer

The last step is to check that the secondment was solid enough to continue with rules, absences and calculation. Here the goal is not only to have filled fields, but to have left a clear structure that the engine can interpret unambiguously.

Before you finish, make sure that:
1. You've already checked the structural secondment of the key profiles.
2. You already distinguish secondment, habilitation and assignment.
3. You've already validated that the visible collective is the right one.
4. You've corrected the main misalignments.

To confirm that the secondment is already ready:
1. Go back to the general list of drivers.
2. Check that the relevant collective for your case appears in the correct context.
3. Check that there are no obvious deposit, unit or group errors.
4. Ask yourself if the system could already:
   1. properly filter the drivers of the case,
   2. apply to them rules of the right collective,
   3. and treat them as a basis for availability and calculation.
5. If the answer is yes, continue with the next quick start.
6. If the answer is no, correct the secondment before continuing.

For the reference case, do not continue until you can state:
1. L1 drivers are attached to the correct context.
2. You know who belongs, who can work, and who's ceded.
3. The base is now ready to apply Rostering rules and availability.

When you finish this section, you should have an operating secondment clear enough to continue with the next layer of the process.
